**EU Plans to Invest Frozen Russian Reserves to Support Ukraine**
The European Commission has proposed transferring nearly 200 billion euros of frozen Russian reserves to a special investment fund that will invest them in more profitable assets. The European Commission now wants to transfer these assets to a new investment fund, a specially created company that can invest in riskier but more profitable instruments. The expected profit from these operations will allow for significantly larger amounts of funding for Ukraine without confiscating the Russian money. Several EU countries, including Germany and Italy, have so far opposed the complete confiscation of Russian reserves, considering it legally questionable and potentially damaging to the trust in the euro as a global currency. Moreover, confiscation could create a precedent that would complicate cooperation with other countries that keep their reserves in European banks. The new approach, which involves only using interest and not the main capital, is a compromise that allows both supporting Ukraine and not violating international law. This new approach also allows avoiding potential blocking of sanctions by Hungary. The European Commission is currently considering creating a special fund without the need for unanimous agreement from all member states, allowing a decision to be made by a simple majority. The EU is already exhausting previously agreed funding for Ukraine: 18 billion euros of the G7 loan are to be paid by the end of 2025, and the centralized aid fund for Ukraine of 50 billion euros, which was to operate until 2027, is quickly dwindling. This is happening against the backdrop of an increasingly tense budget situation in Europe itself. Member countries do not want to increase spending due to internal priorities, and updating the EU budget will only be possible after 2028. Uncertainty about the possible victory of Donald Trump in the US, who has promised to cut aid to Ukraine, forces the EU to prepare a backup plan for financing. It is still unclear in which specific risky instruments the funds will be invested. However, the approach is clear: more income - more stable support for Ukraine. European officials are already negotiating with France, Germany, Italy, and Estonia regarding the legal nuances. However, the working group has not yet presented a final scheme.
