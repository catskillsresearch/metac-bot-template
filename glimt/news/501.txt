Italy has experienced the largest decline in real wages among the main OECD economies, according to the OECD Employment Outlook 2025. In Italy, the renewal of major collective contracts in the past year has led to negotiated salary increases above the usual level. However, these have not been enough to fully compensate for the loss of purchasing power caused by inflation. Furthermore, at the beginning of the first quarter of 2025, one in three private sector employees was still covered by an expired collective contract. The OECD forecasts that real wage growth will remain modest over the next two years, with nominal salaries in Italy expected to increase by 2.6% in 2025 and 2.2% in 2026. The organization also emphasizes the need for labor policies to evolve and help workers remain in the workforce longer, promoting lifelong learning, safe workplaces, flexible pensions, and inclusive practices by employers. The OECD notes that the population in working age in Italy will decrease by 34% between 2023 and 2060, leading to an increase in the number of elderly people per working-age person, from 0.41 to 0.76. This could lead to a decrease in the ratio of employed to total population, and a decrease in the GDP per capita, unless measures are taken to increase the employment of older workers and promote gender equality in the workforce.

**OECD Lowers Global Growth Forecast to Lowest Level Since Pandemic**
The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) has lowered its global growth forecast to the lowest level since the pandemic. The US is expected to be particularly affected. The OECD warns that trade agreements to ease trade tensions would be crucial to reactivate investment and prevent price increases. The US growth is expected to slow down significantly, from 2.8% last year to 1.6% in 2025 and 1.5% in 2026. The OECD also lowered its growth forecasts for 2025 for the G20 countries, including China, France, India, Japan, South Africa, and the UK. The OECD assumes that the US tariffs will remain in place from mid-May. As a result, US inflation is expected to rise to nearly 4% by the end of 2025. The OECD warns of a 'notable cooling' of real GDP growth in the US, along with a significant increase in inflation expectations. The OECD's growth forecast for this year has been reduced for approximately three-quarters of the G20 members compared to its provisional forecast in March. China's growth is expected to slow down from 5% last year to 4.7% in 2025 and 4.3% in 2026. The UK's economy is expected to grow 1.3% this year and 1% in 2026. World trade is expected to grow 2.8% in 2025 and 2.2% in 2026, figures much lower than the OECD's December forecast. The OECD warns that fiscal risks are increasing along with trade tensions, and that demand for increased defense spending will increase spending. A long period of low investment has exacerbated the long-term challenges facing OECD economies, further weakening growth prospects. Companies have avoided investing in fixed capital in favor of accumulating financial assets and returning funds to shareholders. 'The tariffs and uncertainty related to policy have worsened the prospects compared to the March forecast,' said OECD spokesperson Pereira. 'The measures taken by the US have undergone strong fluctuations.' The OECD also warned that the US has increased its effective average tariff rate to over 15%, the highest level since World War II. The OECD assumes that the US will impose a series of sectoral barriers, including increasing tariffs on steel and aluminum imports to 50%.

**G20 Countries' GDP Growth Accelerates in Fourth Quarter of 2024**
The G20 countries' GDP growth accelerated to 0.9% in the fourth quarter of 2024, up from 0.8% in the previous quarter, according to preliminary data from the OECD. In contrast, Japan, South Africa, and Italy had the lowest annual growth rates at 0.1%, 0.6%, and 0.7% respectively. Turkey and South Africa saw significant improvements in their growth rates, while the US, Saudi Arabia, and Brazil experienced a slowdown. Germany was the only country to record a decline in 2024, with a -0.2% growth rate, following a -0.3% decline in 2023. Nine countries had lower growth rates than in 2023. 'The growth repunted notably in Turkey and South Africa during the last quarter of the year,' the OECD added.
